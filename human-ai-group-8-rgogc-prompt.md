# RGOGC Prompt — Human-AI Group 8 (Place a Task)

## R — Role
You are a senior presentation designer and React engineer who builds interactive slides that run live in a lecture room. You care about Apple-grade restraint, legibility from the back row, one obvious action per click, motion that explains rather than decorates, and a presenter who never has to fight the software. You default to removing elements rather than adding them.

## G — Goal
Build one interactive slide for Human-AI Group 8's class presentation in MGMT 6110 (Week 7, The Map of Human-AI Collaboration). The screen shows a 2×2 map of human-AI collaboration and a row of six task cards. The class calls a quadrant out loud, the presenter clicks the card and then the quadrant the class called, and the card glides into the quadrant the group chose. Underneath, the slide says whether the class agreed, then gives the group's one-line reason. By the end all six cards sit on the map and the room has argued about at least one of them.

## O — Output
A Vite + React + TypeScript project in `human-ai-group-8/` with plain CSS, a README, and `node:test` tests for the placement logic. One screen, top to bottom:

1. **Eyebrow** — centred, in Apple's orange eyebrow style, sentence case: "Place a task · Your turn". A tiny grey key hint, "Press F for full screen · D for dark", sits in the top-right corner until full screen is on.
2. **Title** — centred, one line: "Where would you place each of these tasks, and who should lead it?"
3. **Subtitle** — "Call the quadrant for each task before we click. The placements are our group's view, and you may well place some differently." This is the only place the slide tells the audience what to do. Once all six cards are placed, the same line fades into a closing question in semibold pink-to-blue gradient type, in the way apple.com highlights a phrase: "All six are placed. Which one would you move, and why?" It goes back to the instruction when any card returns to the tray.
4. **Main tile** — one large Apple-grey tile with generously rounded corners and no border. Inside, top left, a small near-black pill badge: "Human-AI Group 8". In the middle, a small colour key: a pink person icon "A person leads" and a blue sparkles icon "AI does the work", so the room sees that the colours split the map diagonally by who is in charge. Top right, six progress dots like the iPhone home-screen page dots: grey when empty, and as each card lands the next dot fills in the colour of the quadrant it landed in. Screen readers hear "Click a task", then "3 of 6 placed", then "All 6 placed" instead.
5. **The map** — inside the panel, a 2×2 grid with these labels, exactly as in the course slide:
   - Column headings: "Work with a right answer" (left), "Work with no single right answer" (right).
   - Row headings: "Work that people and AI must do together" (top), "Work that either of them can do alone" (bottom).
   - Quadrants: top left "A person leads, AI inside" (pink), top right "Agents run it, a person judges" (blue), bottom left "AI alone, with checks" (blue), bottom right "A person decides, AI alongside" (pink).
   Column and row headings share one quiet grey (`#6e6e73`, semibold), like the labels in an Apple spec table, so the coloured quadrant names carry the hierarchy.
   Each quadrant is a softly tinted rounded tile with no border. Its name sits at the top left in semibold, in the quadrant's colour, after the same icon as the key: a person on pink quadrants, sparkles on blue ones, and placed cards stack below it as white chips.
6. **Reason panel** — a white rounded tile under the map with a slim rounded bar inside its left edge: light grey at the start, Apple blue while a card waits for the class's call, then the colour of the quadrant the shown card sits in, so the eye links the reason to the card. Before anything is placed, a muted "Pick a task below to start." While a card waits for the call: "Where does the class put **Market surveying**? Click that quadrant, or click the card again to reveal without a call." After the reveal the panel has two levels, as on apple.com: a small top line with the task name in semibold, a grey dot and the verdict, then the group's reason below in larger type. The verdict reads "The class agreed: Y" in green after a filled check-circle icon, "The class called X. We placed it in Y." in Apple orange after a two-way swap-arrows icon when the room differed, or "Placed in Y" when no call was recorded. The quadrant that just received a card glows briefly.
7. **Task tray** — the six cards as white rounded chips with a hairline edge and a soft shadow, in one wrapping row, followed by an Apple-blue pill "Show all" button and a blue outlined pill "Clear" button. Once all six are placed, both buttons give way to a single blue text link, "Start over ›", in the style of apple.com's "Learn more ›" links. A placed card leaves a faint grey chip in its slot with its name in light grey, so the tray never reflows and never reads as a row of blank boxes.
8. **Footer** — a hairline rule and small grey type, then "SMU · MGMT 6110 · Week 7 · The Map of Human-AI Collaboration" on the left and "Human-AI Group 8" on the right, each on one line. When the group lists its members in `src/tasks.ts`, their names follow "Human-AI Group 8", for example "Human-AI Group 8: Name, Name, Name".
9. **README** — run it locally, how to edit the tasks and reasons, presenting tips (clicker keys, the F key for full screen), deploy to Vercel, project layout, a short "Next" list.

**The six tasks, their quadrants and reasons** (reasons are drafts for the group to edit in `src/tasks.ts`):

| Card | Quadrant | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Schedule-making assistant | Agents run it, a person judges | The agent can juggle calendars, travel time and clashes on its own, but only you know which meetings really matter, so you approve the week it proposes. |
| Cancelling a subscription | Agents run it, a person judges | The agent can find the account and work through the cancel flow, but it stops for your confirmation before anything is final, because money and access are at stake. |
| Market surveying | A person leads, AI inside | AI drafts the questions and tallies the answers fast, but a person decides what we need to learn and checks that the numbers hold up. |
| News briefing | AI alone, with checks | Summarising today's headlines is well-defined work AI can do on its own, with spot checks against the original sources. |
| Developing a business product and what to sell | A person leads, AI inside | AI can size demand, compare competitors and stress-test ideas, but a person owns the bet on what to sell and answers for it. |
| Outfit planner | A person decides, AI alongside | There is no single right outfit. AI can suggest combinations from your wardrobe and the weather, but taste is personal, so you choose. |

## G — Guardrails
- **Stack:** Vite, React 19, TypeScript, plain CSS. No UI kit, no animation library, no state library, no icon package, no backend, no analytics, no runtime network requests, no web-font downloads. It must run from the laptop with the Wi-Fi off.
- **Interaction:** clicking a tray card lifts it 5px as a pane of frosted glass (a translucent white gradient, 16px backdrop blur and a bright top edge) with a blue ring and a soft blue shadow, and every quadrant gains a faint blue inner ring and becomes clickable. Clicking the quadrant the class called records that call and places the card in the group's quadrant. Clicking the lifted card again reveals it without a call, and Escape puts it down. The class's call is forgotten when a card goes back to the tray. Clicking a placed card shows its reason again, so the room can revisit any card mid-discussion. Pointing at a placed card reveals a small frosted-glass × badge at its corner, like an iOS delete badge, and clicking the × sends the card back to its slot in the tray. If its reason was showing, the panel shows the card placed before it, or the starting text when none is left. Keyboard focus follows the card: after placing, it moves to the next tray card. "Show all" places the remaining cards one after another, 150 ms apart, and the reason panel then shows the last one. "Clear" sends every card home and restores the starting text. All cards are real `<button>`s. Every control, the cards included, shrinks to 97% while pressed, as on iOS.
- **Clicker support:** Right arrow, Down arrow, Page Down and Space reveal the lifted card, or else place the next unplaced card in tray order, without a call. Left arrow, Up arrow and Page Up put down the lifted card, or else return the most recently placed card. Presentation clickers send exactly these keys. F toggles browser full screen and D toggles the dark theme. A small grey "Press F for full screen · D for dark" sits in the top-right corner until full screen is on, and says "D for light" while dark. Nothing else listens to the keyboard.
- **Motion:** the card flies from its tray slot to its quadrant over 560 ms on an Apple-style spring: it overshoots its spot slightly and settles, lifts to 106% size a third of the way through, and its shadow fades as it lands. It is a FLIP animation on the separate `translate` and `scale` properties, not a layout animation. The receiving quadrant glows with a soft ring in its own colour for 900 ms. Returning cards fly back the same way. Under `prefers-reduced-motion`, cards move instantly and nothing glows.
- **State:** in memory only. A reload is a clean reset, which is what a presenter wants before class. No localStorage.
- **Typography:** San Francisco throughout, via `-apple-system, BlinkMacSystemFont, "SF Pro Display", "SF Pro Text", "Helvetica Neue", Helvetica, Arial, sans-serif`, the way apple.com sets it. Headlines semibold with tight tracking (-0.02em), body regular with slight negative tracking, no all-caps anywhere. Title about 33px and body about 18px at 1280px wide, scaling with the slide. Cards are the largest text after the title: about 19px in the tray and 20px on the map at 1280px, 29px and 31px at 1920px, so the back row can read every one.
- **Colour:** Apple's palette. Page `#ffffff`, tile `#f5f5f7`, text `#1d1d1f`, secondary `#6e6e73`, tertiary `#86868b`, hairlines `#d2d2d7`, primary blue `#0071e3`, eyebrow orange `#bf4800`, agree green `#248a3d`, differ orange `#bf4800`. Pink quadrants `#fdf0f5` with names in `#c8105a`; blue quadrants `#eef5fd` with names in `#0066cc`. No hard borders: separation comes from tints, rounded corners and soft shadows. A dark theme, after Apple's dark product pages, is one key press away for dim rooms: page `#000000`, tile `#161617`, chips `#2c2c2e`, text `#f5f5f7`, pink `#ff6b9d`, blue `#2997ff`, green `#30d158`, orange `#ff9f0a`. Light by default. The dark theme changes only when the presenter presses D: the laptop's own dark mode must never flip the slide on the projector. Colours sit in tokens on `:root`. 4.5:1 contrast for all text.
- **Layout:** a fixed 16:9 stage, like a Keynote slide. Every size is a fraction of the stage width, so the slide is the same picture at any window size: it scales to the largest 16:9 box that fits, centred and letterboxed in white, and the page never scrolls in any direction. There is no stacked mobile layout. On a phone held upright, one grey line under the slide says "Turn your phone sideways for a bigger view." The two map rows share the height equally, and a row grows only when its cards need the room, so a two-line card never gets clipped. Corner radii: about 26px on the main tile, 18px on quadrants and the reason tile, 15px on chips, full pills on buttons and the badge at 1280px.
- **Glass:** frosted glass appears on those two elements only, the lifted card and the × badge, after Apple's newest design language. Nowhere else, because low-contrast effects can wash out on a projector; check it on the real projector before class.
- **Copy:** the axis and quadrant labels match the course slide word for word. The group speaks as "we" and "our". No exclamation marks, no emoji.
- **Do not add:** thick coloured edge bars on tiles, dashed borders, monospace or all-caps type; Prof. Roh's name, badge, watermark or copyright line; his tasks, placements or reasons; the deck's step navigation ("Welcome back · The map · …"); a slide number; scoring; voting on phones or laptops; timers; sound; confetti; drag and drop; following the OS dark mode; PWA install; settings.
- **Quality bar:** `npm run build` passes the type check, `npm test` passes, no console errors or warnings, works offline, fits every window shape with no scroll, works with a clicker or keyboard alone, and every card lands in the right quadrant.
